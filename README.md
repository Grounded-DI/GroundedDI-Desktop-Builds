# 2. **riefWise DI² — Offline Legal-Authority Replay Demonstration**

BriefWise retrieved a public court opinion from CourtListener, froze the acquired source, and recorded separate hashes for:

- transport content;
- decoded content; and
- normalized legal authority.

The matter was then closed and reopened while BriefWise was offline, with no live provider access.

BriefWise reconstructed the trusted matter from frozen evidence, reran the governed rule-based evaluation, regenerated the canonical bytes, and reproduced the same SHA-256 replay identity.

After network access was restored, BriefWise rechecked the upstream source and classified the result as:

> **UNCHANGED TRANSPORT AND CONTENT**

## What Was Demonstrated

The receipt records four distinct operations within one governed matter:

1. **Live acquisition and freezing**  
   CourtListener material was retrieved, selected, frozen locally, and preserved for offline use.

2. **Fresh offline replay**  
   Closing and reopening triggered a fresh reconstruction and evaluation. BriefWise did not rely on stored `PASS` text.

3. **Canonical identity reproduction**  
   The regenerated canonical bytes and SHA-256 replay identity matched the previously recorded values.

4. **Independent legal-sufficiency state**  
   The matter remained:

   > **PROP UNSUPPORTED**

   This shows that source integrity does not automatically establish proposition support, legal sufficiency, good-law status, or FilingReady authorization.

## Demonstrated Control Sequence

```text
Live legal-authority retrieval
        ↓
Attorney-bounded selection
        ↓
Frozen evidentiary source
        ↓
Offline close and reopen
        ↓
Fresh governed evaluation
        ↓
Canonical-byte and SHA-256 identity reproduction
        ↓
Live upstream source recheck
        ↓
Change classification
        ↓
Separate legal-sufficiency and release determination
````

## What This Test Does Not Claim

This demonstration does **not** establish that:

* an AI model reproduced hidden reasoning;
* a probabilistic model generated identical prose;
* retrieval established current treatment or good-law status;
* the opinion supported the matter’s legal proposition;
* the matter was FilingReady; or
* every BriefWise execution will always produce identical artifacts.

The demonstrated result is narrower:

> BriefWise reproduced the same governed legal evaluation and canonical identity from frozen evidence under the tested offline replay conditions.

## Public Position

We are not aware of a previously documented legal-AI workflow demonstrating this complete sequence within one governed matter state.

The demonstration combines established retrieval, digital-evidence preservation, content-addressing, fresh recomputation, and replay techniques within a distinct legal authorization workflow.

This statement is limited to the publicly documented sequence described above and is not a claim that the underlying component technologies are individually new.
